Intensity and effective path

Centre beam. Intensity Rʲ after j reflections (left axis, log) and the absorption-weighted path Leff(j) = Σ Rⁱ ℓᵢ accumulated so far (right axis). Leff tends to ℓ̄ / (1 − R) for long traces; a weak absorber α reduces the integrated signal by α Leff.

Mirror reflectance over the cell's angles

Bars: share of the light's power arriving at the mirrors per 1° of |χ| (each hit weighted by the intensity it still carries), this cell. Grey outline: the same launch in the unperturbed cell. Line, right axis: mirror loss 1 − R(χ) of the DBR. Above the critical angle the first air gap reflects totally (only frustrated TIR through the gap leaks).

Every reflection uses the exact transfer-matrix R(χ) at the angle that ray meets, so a ray's intensity is the product of the reflectances it met. R per bounce is I_end^(1/n), the decay of the total power (a geometric mean over hits would count hits of rays already killed); ⟨R⟩ is weighted by the power arriving at each hit. Closed cell: the beams and reflections set in the Beams panel, ports ignored. Mirror: bragg_grating.TrenchDBR, membrane | [air gap, tooth] × N | air, 1D effective index, λ from the phase panel. Engine: math-engines/engines/cell_mirror.

Phase space at the mirrors

Each hit plotted as boundary position s/P against sin χ. Regular orbits trace lines or points; chaotic ones fill areas.

Twin-ray separation

The centre beam and a copy launched 1 nrad apart, ports closed. A straight rise on this log scale means exponential divergence (chaos); the dashed fit gives the Lyapunov exponent per reflection. Tilting flat facets only gives slow, linear growth: a polygon stays non-chaotic, so add facet curvature for true chaos.

Phase vs launch angle, and angle dither

Relative phase Δφp(θ) = k₀ n (Lp(θ) − Lp(θc))

Shaded band: the dither range ±A. Signed-log axis, so sub-wave slopes and mm-scale path jumps both show.

Visibility Vp against pass number

Dots: computed V. Dashed: linear-model prediction from the dither depth xp. Grey: share of dithered rays still on the centre ray's facet path. Dotted: random-phase floor √π/(2√N).

Visibility against dither depth x = apA (every pass)

The curve is |J₀(x)| (sine) or |sinc(x)| (triangle). Passes whose dots sit on it are explained by x alone. Amber: some rays switched facet path. Hollow red: not resolved at the maximum sample count. Ringed: the pass shown.

Phase slope |ap| = k₀ n |dLp/dθ| against pass number

Horizontal lines: the slope at which the current amplitude A gives model V = 0.9, 0.5 and 0. A pass is washed out where the curve is above the V = 0 line. Changing A moves these lines; changing λ, n or the cell moves the curve.

How each parameter changes the visibility
  • Dither amplitude A: x ∝ A. Doubling A doubles x at every pass.
  • Wavelength and index: x ∝ n/λ through k₀n. A membrane mode with neff ≈ 2.8 washes out 2.8 times sooner than air.
  • Pass number: dLp/dθ accumulates along the path. In a regular polygon it grows in proportion to p (fitted exponent γ ≈ 1), so the pass where V first reaches 0 moves as 1/A. In a chaotic (curved-facet) cell it grows exponentially, and washing out sets in abruptly after a few Lyapunov times.
  • Cell size and launch angle: at fixed shape dL/dθ ∝ radius; θc sets the chord geometry and the sign and size of the slope (near normal incidence the first-chord slope vanishes).
  • Waveform: sine dither gives |J₀(x)| (V = 0.9 at x = 0.64, 0.5 at 1.52, first zero 2.40, then a slow 1/√x tail with revivals). Triangle dither gives |sinc(x)| (0.9 at 0.79, 0.5 at 1.90, zero at π, 1/x tail). A triangle suppresses more strongly at large x; a sine reaches its first zero sooner.
  • Chirp ½|bp|A²: curvature of the phase across the dither. Once it nears 1 rad, V no longer follows the J₀/sinc curve.
  • Path switching (perturbations, large A, late passes): dithered rays that take a different facet path differ by a chord or more, thousands of waves. Their contribution averages to nearly nothing. The rays that keep the path are those near δ = 0, so they behave like a smaller effective dither; V then depends on where along the dither the switching happens, not on x alone, and can lie above or below the J₀/sinc curve.
  • Sampling: the dither is sampled until the phase step between neighbouring same-path samples is below π/2 at every pass, up to 12 801 samples. An unresolved V is only known to be about as small as the random-phase floor.

Vp = |time average of exp(iΔφp)| over one dither period: the share of an interference term between pass-p light and light that does not follow the dither that survives averaging. V = 1 keeps fringes; V → 0 washes them out. Ray picture, reflection phases ignored. Engine: math-engines/engines/ray_phase (dither_analysis); runs in a background worker.

Geometry and reflection law are a JavaScript port of SegmentedCell in misc-applets math-engines/engines/billiard_cell, checked against the Python engine's test vectors. Flat or circular-arc facets, extended 15 % past the polygon corners; 2D ray optics with specular reflection.
