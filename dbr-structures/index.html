Model and limits

  • Effective-index method. Each slice of a period is replaced by the fundamental slab mode of its local core thickness t − h(1 − g). Mode mismatch scattering at the tooth walls and radiation inside the stop band are not in the transfer matrix.
  • Coupling coefficient. κ = 2π|Nm|/λ, where Nm is the m-th Fourier coefficient of neff(z). For a rectangular profile this is 2Δn sin(πmf)/(mλ). Even orders vanish at f = 0.5.
  • Spectrum. Abelès matrices for every slice, the period matrix raised to the power N. Input and output guides have the tooth index. Propagation loss, and radiation loss of higher-order gratings at λ₀, enter as an imaginary index.
  • Scattering. The etched layer is a current sheet Δε = ncore² − nclad² of thickness h at depth t − h/2, driven by the probe mode. αq = k⁴(Δε)²h²|Gq|²φ²/(4βkx). Reflections from the slab interfaces are ignored, so up/down ratios are rough; expect agreement with FDTD within a factor of a few for shallow gratings.
  • Far field. One period's emission times the array factor of N periods with exponential decay from radiation and loss. Lobe widths follow from min(L, 1/α).
  • SHG in the grating. Pump amplitudes Ap, Bp (forward, backward) at every period boundary come from the transfer matrix; inside a period U = A eiβz + B e−iβz. PNL = ε₀deffU²ψ² in the core only. Radiated SH treats the core as a sheet (no phase variation across the thickness, no slab Fresnel effects); guided SH uses the overlap ∫ψSHψF² dx and coupled-mode theory, no pump depletion. The A·B term gives emission normal to the surface even without a grating harmonic. 2D powers per unit width are multiplied by the effective width w. Checked: with h = 0 the guided SH reproduces the sinc²(ΔkL/2) law exactly.
  • CRIGF. Coupler by Kazarinov–Henry coupled-mode theory: forward and backward envelopes with radiative self- and cross-coupling (the two waves radiate coherently near the normal), 2nd-order guided Bragg coupling from the neff modulation, and Gaussian-beam excitation through the local field at the grating, found from the bare slab. DBRs and spacers enter as exact transfer-matrix reflections at the coupler ends. Out-coupling into the reflected and transmitted beams follows by reciprocity from the same coupling constant, so the model conserves energy: with no loss, R + T + escape = 1 to 10⁻⁴ for the coupler alone. On resonance the remainder is light radiated into spatial modes other than the incident beam (mode mismatch), shown as its own curve; it is smallest when 2w₀ is close to the coupler length.
  • Straight poled sections. Between the coupler and each DBR, length Ls plus the phase spacer. The pump there is A eiβz + B e−iβz with loss, from the cavity solution. The SHG integral over periodic domains is a geometric series, so millimetre sections cost nothing extra. Guided SH accumulates phase region by region with each region's own SH index, and the straight sections use their own Δk and poling period. A·B (counter-propagating) terms emit near the normal, or at sin θ = mKpp/(k₂n) when poled. The resonance finder maximises circulating power over one free spectral range; the sweep also tunes the phase spacer, because at normal incidence the coupler can sit on a node of the radiating combination (a dark mode). No pump depletion or SH loading of the cavity: results above about 10 % conversion overestimate.
  • QPM. Domain walls from the local poling wavevector K(z) = K₀ + c(z − L/2) and duty D(z) with sin(πD) following a raised-cosine taper. η(λ) = |gΩ∫s(z)eiΔk zdz|²/w with the same overlaps as the SHG model; Δk(λ) from the slab solver at every wavelength. The optimizer runs a secant search on the chirp for each taper length and keeps the design with the least in-band ripple at the target FWHM.
  • Lateral width. Ridge, rib or strip-loaded guide: a second effective-index step across the width W for the unetched ridge (side regions are the slab of thickness tside, or the substrate for a strip); grating slices are added as perturbations weighted by the lateral confinement Γlat, which stays valid for shallow strip loading where etched slots are thinner than the sides, so contrast, κ, Bragg wavelength, Δk and QPM period all follow W. The lateral mode sets the beam overlap (power overlap with the Gaussian beam) and the SHG width 1/∫φ⁴dy. Open slab: the grating lines are circular arcs of radius Rc and width A; the lateral field is a Gaussian beam. A propagate–reflect–propagate (ABCD) round trip with clipping at the aperture gives the bounce efficiency η that scales the DBR amplitude reflection by √η in the CRIGF, with the lateral waist chosen to maximise η (it equals the resonator eigenmode when the cavity is stable). The mirror plane sits one DBR penetration depth tanh(κL)/2κ inside the grating.
  • Vertical stack. Optional handle under the substrate layer (then a BOX of thickness tBOX): Si (light radiated downward continues into the Si) or a gold mirror (the downward channel is closed). Plane waves are solved through the whole stack, so BOX interference sets the coupler's up/down ratio and its strength. The guided mode is assumed not to leak into the handle (thick enough BOX).
  • Pump depletion. From the undepleted solution, ENL = guided SH per direction / Pc². Per pass a fraction tanh²(√(ENLPc)) converts; it enters the straight sections as extra loss, and Pc is iterated with the full cavity solver until consistent. Radiated SH keeps its low-power ratio to the guided SH. Not included: Kerr and thermal detuning, photorefractive index drift, absorption of the SH (GRIIRA/BLIIRA), SH feedback by the DBRs.
  • Photorefraction. Peak guided SH intensity = (SH per output) / (mode area), area = lateral SHG width/√2 × vertical 1/∫φ⁴. Onset thresholds are order-of-magnitude placeholders: undoped congruent LN shows index drift at kW/cm² visible, MgO:LN near MW/cm², LT about ten times above that. Measured TFLN/TFLT values depend on doping, stoichiometry, annealing and cladding, so edit them.
  • Materials. Sellmeier fits, isotropic, absorption ignored. Si and most III-V cores absorb at visible SHG wavelengths. For custom layers, the index is a Cauchy curve through the values you give at λ₀ and at the probe wavelength.
