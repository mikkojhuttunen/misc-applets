Er:Al₂O₃ strip-loaded waveguide amplifier

An erbium-doped Al₂O₃ strip loads a thin film of Al₂O₃, Si₃N₄, LiNbO₃ (TFLN) or LiTaO₃ (TFLT) on SiO₂, with a SiO₂ top cladding or air above. The pump and signal modes are solved for the cross-section, their overlaps with the doped strip feed steady-state erbium rate equations, and the pump and signal are propagated along the device to give the gain spectrum.

Rate equations

Er³⁺ is treated as an effective two-level system (⁴I15/2 ground state with population N₁, ⁴I13/2 metastable level with N₂ = NEr − N₁). At 980 nm the ⁴I11/2 level empties fast into ⁴I13/2, so the pump only absorbs; at 1480 nm the pump also causes stimulated emission. Energy-transfer upconversion removes excitations at the rate CupN₂². In steady state, in every cell of the doped strip,

0 = (Rp,a + Rs,a) N₁ − (Rp,e + Rs,e) N₂ − N₂/τ − CupN₂², R = σ I/(hν), I(x,y) = P |E(x,y)|² / ∫|E|² dA

Propagation and overlaps

dPp/dz = −[ ∫(σa,pN₁ − σe,pN₂)|Ep|² dA / ∫|Ep|² dA + α ] Pp
dPs/dz = [ ∫(σe,sN₂ − σa,sN₁)|Es|² dA / ∫|Es|² dA − α ] Ps

The integrals run over the doped strip only, so for uniform inversion they reduce to Γ(σeN₂ − σaN₁) with the overlap Γ = ∫strip|E|² dA / ∫|E|² dA. Here the inversion is computed locally, so the bright centre of the pump mode inverts more than the edges of the strip. The probe gain spectrum uses the inversion set up by the pump and the signal:

G(λ) [dB] = 4.343 [σe(λ) ∫n̄₂ dz − σa(λ) ∫n̄₁ dz] · Γs(λ)/Γs(λs) − α L, n̄ = ∫N|Es|² dA / ∫|Es|² dA

Spectroscopy and limits of the model

The absorption cross-section is a smooth model of the Er:Al₂O₃ band (peak at 1533 nm, adjustable height); the emission cross-section follows from McCumber theory, σe(λ) = σa(λ) exp[(hc/λ₀ − hc/λ)/kT] with λ₀ = 1532 nm and T = 295 K. Default values are typical of sputtered Al₂O₃:Er films (σa(1533) ≈ 5.7×10⁻²¹ cm², σa(980) ≈ 1.7×10⁻²¹ cm², τ ≈ 7.5 ms, Cup a few 10⁻¹⁸ cm³/s); use measured data for your films where you have them. With concentration effects on, the upconversion coefficient grows in proportion to NEr (Cup = Cup(10²⁰)·NEr/10²⁰) and a fraction fq of the ions is quenched, either growing with concentration (fq = kqNEr, clustering) or fixed (set by the deposition process). Quenched ions decay too fast to be inverted (sub-µs instead of ms), so they stay in the ground state and only absorb pump and signal; the remaining ions follow the rate equation above. Quenching and concentration-dependent upconversion are what make an optimum concentration appear. The "Gain vs quenching" view sweeps the quenched share and, for each value, also searches the best NEr (0.1–10×10²⁰ cm⁻³) and length (up to 30 cm), which shows how much a lower-quenching film is worth once the design is adapted to it. Not included: amplified spontaneous emission (it limits gain above roughly 20–30 dB), excited-state absorption, partly inverted ion pairs, and Er in the film (only the strip is doped). The background loss α is taken equal at pump and signal. Modes come from the effective index method or the semi-vectorial 2D finite-difference solver of the waveguide mode explorer; Γs(λ) for the spectrum is evaluated with the EIM at 25 nm steps and scaled to the selected solver at λs.
