Gas Cell Ray Tracer

An elongated on-chip Herriott cell with smooth, deformable mirrors against a circular multipass cell (CMPC) of the same footprint, both fed by the same Gaussian beam. The Herriott cell keeps one clean beam on a long, stable path; the CMPC scrambles its rays, which washes out interference fringes but spreads the beam. Read off the path you gain against the chaos you lose, and what that means for the detection limit of a gas line. It opens on the lowest-fringe design found: a 10 × 1.5 mm Herriott cell with closed side walls, 0.3 µm mirror ripple at Λ = 0.5 mm (K ≈ 0.95) and six index inclusions (r = 50 µm, |Δn| = 0.01, seed 4), with a 2 mrad dither. It keeps about 80 % of the light over a 31 cm path; its exit fringe is below the error of the dither integral (a few 10⁻³), and the detector averages it further over about M² ≈ 70 speckle grains. Set the inclusions and the ripple to 0 for the ideal cell.

Baseline: elongated Herriott cell
Mirror deformation

A deformed mirror moves the spot pattern off re-entrance; re-aiming scans ± 4 beam divergences around the design angle.

Chaos makers: segmented mirrors

Curvature 1 continues the mirror (no kinks); 0 gives flat facets; other values put a slope jump at every joint that splits neighbouring rays, as the facets of the circular cell do.

Chaos makers: rippled end mirrors
Stochasticity K = 8π² a L / Λ²0

A ripple a sin(2πη/Λ) on the mirror kicks each reflected ray by 2aq cos(qη), q = 2π/Λ; the flight over L turns the kicks into a standard map with K = 8π²aL/Λ². Near K ≈ 1 the last regular barriers break: about 0.3 µm at Λ = 0.5 mm and L = 10 mm. Whether the beam then lands in the chaotic sea or on a surviving island depends on where it starts (window position, launch angle, ripple phase): see the chaos map. Smooth mirrors only (segments 0).

Chaos makers: index inclusions

Small disks of slightly different effective index (shallow etch, local cladding): rays refract at their edges and gain optical path inside. In an open cell the deflected light leaves through the sides; close the side walls to keep it.

Model and definitions
  • Baseline: two concave in-plane mirrors at spacing L, aperture W, re-entrant design R = L/(1 − cos 2πM/N), one window on M1 for input and output. Each mirror is a smooth profile ξ = cη²/(1 + √(1 − (1+k)c²η²)) + a₃η³ + a₄η⁴ with a₄ = ε₄ c/(2a²) and a₃ = ε₃ c/(2a) (a = W/2), so ε is the extra sag at the edge as a share of a²/(2R); rays meet it exactly (safeguarded Newton).
  • Comparison: a regular polygon of flat facets (the CMPC) or a smooth circle with the same area, input port mid facet 0, output port further along the wall.
  • Input beam: the eigenmode of the undeformed baseline at its window, sampled as an n × n grid of rays from its Wigner function (normal quantiles in offset and angle, wavefront curvature included). Both cells get the same beam.
  • Path: the rays are traced port to port; each reflection multiplies by R. T = detected power, ⟨L⟩ its power-weighted mean path, absorbance per ppm A = 100 α Γ ⟨L⟩ with α of the strongest listed line (illustrative data, ±30 %).
  • Detection limit: shot noise √(2qB/(P T)) with 1 A/W and 1 Hz; fringe floor 2√f V_det, f the parasitic power fraction that interferes with the signal and V the visibility left after the launch-angle dither; NEC = √(shot² + fringe²)/A.
  • Chaos: Lyapunov exponent = slope of ln(twin separation) per hit over its exponential stretch (0: none found); dither visibility V = |time average of exp(iΔφ)| of the exit pass.
  • Dither integral (adaptive Filon): the dither range is cut into intervals, on each of which the phase is taken as linear between its ends and integrated exactly, however many waves it turns; intervals whose phase is not yet linear enough (second difference > 1 rad, or halving changes the result by more than 10⁻⁷ of its width) are halved, widest first, until the ray budget is spent. A regular or mildly chaotic cell is resolved completely and V is exact far below the old random-phase floor √π/(2√n) (to about 10⁻⁷). After many passes of a strongly chaotic cell the dither range breaks into path segments as fine as 10⁻¹¹ rad, with long-range structure between them; there the open intervals add an error that falls only as 1/√(budget), as in plain sampling, and V below it is reported as an upper bound V + V_err.
  • Detector speckle averaging (option): the scrambled exit light carries about M² independent speckle grains; a detector that collects all of it against a parasitic copy with a smooth wavefront averages the fringe to V_det = V/√M². Without it (parasitic copy with the same speckle) V_det = V.
  • Reaching the shot-noise floor: the fringe stays below the shot noise when 2√f V_det < NEC_shot·A, i.e. V_det ≲ 7·10⁻⁶ for f = 10⁻⁶ at 1 mW (NEC_shot ≈ 14 ppb). That level is far below what any ray sampling of a fully chaotic exit can resolve (it would take about 10¹⁰ rays); the levers that reach it are the parasitic fraction f (V_det ∝ 1/√f at a fixed NEC), the input power (NEC_shot ∝ 1/√P) and speckle averaging, while a regular cell's exact V shows how a dither tuned to a J0 zero of the exit pass can null its fringe.
  • Gaussian-beam two-mirror model: q propagated along the traced centre ray, mirror term −2κ/cos χ with the local curvature κ; per hit the beam radius and the share of its footprint left on the reflecting element (not past the mirror end, not in a window). "Power kept in one Gaussian beam" is the product of those shares and R; |m| = |A + D|/2 of each round trip (two chords), above 1 the orbit is locally unstable.
  • Segmented mirrors: each mirror is cut into K circular arcs whose ends lie on the mirror profile; each arc has the mirror's local curvature times a factor (1: no kinks, 0: flat facets), and optionally a random tilt about its centre. The Gaussian model loses the share of the beam that falls past a segment edge.
  • Index inclusions: disks of radius r and effective index n_eff ± Δn placed at random (fixed seed) without overlap, clear of the mirrors. Rays refract at their edges (Snell; total internal reflection when it applies; Fresnel reflection neglected) and accumulate optical path n ℓ inside, which the phase and dither analysis use; the absorption uses the geometric path. The Gaussian (ABCD) model sees the mirrors only; the M² of the detected light, from the second moments of the exit offsets and angles of the ray bundle (1 for the input beam), shows how much a cell scrambles the beam.
  • Rippled end mirrors: the profile gains a sin(2πη/Λ + φ); rays meet it by sampling every Λ/8 along the chord, bracketing the first crossing and refining it with safeguarded Newton (the rippled mirror may be locally convex). K = 8π²aL/Λ² is the stochasticity of the equivalent standard map.
  • Chaos map: the exact tangent map of the walls, unfolded (flight [[1, ℓ], [0, 1]], reflection [[1, 0], [−2κ/cos χ, 1]] with the local curvature κ including the ripple), renormalised every hit: finite-time Lyapunov exponent and MEGNO ⟨Y⟩ (→ 0 periodic, → 2 regular torus, growing ≈ λn/2 in chaos; > 4 after 300 hits counts as chaotic). Verdict: the beam escapes (half or more of its rays leave within 30 hits), launches into the sea (half or more of the rest chaotic), launches onto an island (regular, while at least 5 % of the bounded section is chaotic), or there is no sea. Flat facets have κ = 0, so a polygon reads regular here: its mixing comes from ray splitting at corners, which the twin rays show. Inclusions and windows are left out of the map.
  • Fringe study behind the defaults (closed 10 × 1.5 mm cell, made with plain dither sampling, floor 3.9·10⁻³): the dither amplitude alone does not lower V. With the ripple only, V stays at 0.07–0.10 for 2–8 mrad, because once the dither decorrelates the paths, the exit fringe is a sum of random phasors. A few strong inclusions do lower it. Six disks with |Δn| = 0.01 put V at or below the floor for most placements, at a cost of a few per cent in power. Ripple plus inclusions was the most robust: the default design stays at the floor for 2, 5 and 8 mrad and for |Δn| = 0.008–0.012 and r = 50–60 µm. Weak inclusions (|Δn| = 0.002) barely help, and the result depends on the placement seed: single designs can be at the floor or at 0.1.
  • Strong chaos: with a Lyapunov exponent above a few per cent per hit, single trajectories are not reproducible beyond tens of hits (round-off grows exponentially); the ensemble numbers are.
  • Not included: diffraction beyond the paraxial Gaussian model, interference between different rays, angle-dependent mirror reflectance, scattering, strong-line saturation.
