Fiber parametric amplifier

A 532 nm pump amplifies a signal near 1550 nm in a second-order nonlinear fiber and creates an idler near 810 nm. The coupled amplitude equations are integrated with RK4, including pump depletion, phase mismatch, Gaussian mode overlap and optional dissipation of the idler.

Bandwidth optimiser

Searches for settings whose signal −3 dB band is a given factor wider than a perfectly phase-matched (Δk = 0), lossless fiber. The −3 dB band must be continuous, with no dip below −3 dB. The search uses undepleted analytic spectra (Nelder–Mead with restarts). The result is applied to the controls, and the full numerical spectrum then re-checks it.

Model

Amplitudes aj(z) are normalised so that |aj|² is the photon flux (photons/s) in mode j. With a continuous-wave, slowly varying envelope and a single transverse mode per wave:

dap/dz = iK as ai e−iΔkz
das/dz = iK ap ai* e+iΔkz
dai/dz = iK ap as* e+iΔkz − (αi/2) ai
K = 2 deff √( ħ ωp ωs ωi / (2 ε0 c³ np ns ni) ) · Θ
Θ = ∫fpfsfi dA / √(∫fp² ∫fs² ∫fi²) = π / (Σ wj−2) / √((π/2)³ wp² ws² wi²)

χ(3) mode (degenerate four-wave mixing, 2ωp = ωs + ωi). With power amplitudes Bj (|B|² in W), γj = n2ωj/(c Aeff), and Δβ = βs + βi − 2βp:

dBp/dz = iγp[(|Bp|² + 2|Bs|² + 2|Bi|²)Bp + 2BsBiBp* eiΔβz]
dBs/dz = iγs[(|Bs|² + 2|Bp|² + 2|Bi|²)Bs + Bp² Bi* e−iΔβz]
dBi/dz = iγi[(|Bi|² + 2|Bp|² + 2|Bs|²)Bi + Bp² Bs* e−iΔβz] − (αi/2)Bi
1/Aeff = ∫fp²fsfi dA / ((π/2)² wp² ws wi) = π / (2wp−2 + ws−2 + wi−2) / ((π/2)² wp² ws wi)

With γj ∝ ωj, two pump photons become one signal and one idler photon exactly, so Φp + 2Φs is conserved even with idler loss. The net mismatch is κ = Δβ + 2γpPp, set by the Δk slider at λs. The fiber is taken to be dispersion-engineered (PCF, birefringence or intermodal matching) so that κ at λs has this value; its own linear Δβ is shown in the readouts. For an undepleted pump, the signal–idler pair obeys the same 2×2 system as in χ(2) with Γ = γpPp√(ωsωi)/ωp and Δk → κ. The bandwidth physics is therefore the same: the mismatch away from λs again follows the signal–idler GVM (β1,i − β1,s) and β2,s + β2,i. Stimulated Raman and Brillouin scattering are not modelled. For narrow-linewidth CW pumps of several watts, Brillouin limits the usable length unless the pump is phase-modulated.

Here fj = exp(−r²/wj²) and Aeff = 1/Θ². The small-signal gain coefficient is Γ = K√Φp(0) with Φp = Pp/ħωp. Without loss and with an undepleted pump, G = 1 + (Γ/g)² sinh²(gL), g² = Γ² − (Δk/2)².

The dashed curves solve the linearised signal–idler pair exactly (2×2 matrix exponential, with lumped dumps applied as jumps). With strong continuous idler loss (αi ≫ Γ) the idler follows the signal adiabatically and the signal sees a Lorentzian gain coefficient gs = Γ²αi / ((αi/2)² + Δk²). The gain becomes smaller but much less sensitive to Δk.

Dispersion and bandwidth. For a CW pump, the mismatch seen by a signal at ωs0 + Ω is Δk(Ω) = Δk + [ΔkD(ωs0 + Ω) − ΔkD(ωs0)], where ΔkD(ωs) = kp(ωp) − ks(ωs) − ki(ωp − ωs). In the step-index model each k = ω neff/c comes from the exact scalar LP01 eigenvalue equation U J1(U)/J0(U) = W K1(W)/K0(W) with a fused-silica Sellmeier core and cladding plus a constant Δn, so material and waveguide dispersion are both included. To first order Δk(Ω) ≈ Δk + (β1,i − β1,s)Ω − ½(β2,s + β2,i)Ω². With the gain coefficient Γ, the GVM-limited full width at half maximum is roughly Δω ≈ 4√(Γ ln 2 / L)/|GVM| at high gain and 5.57/(L |GVM|) at low gain. The plotted spectrum is exact within the model.

The idler band follows from ωi = ωp − ωs. It has the same width as the signal band in frequency, but (λi/λs)² ≈ 0.27 times the width in wavelength. The idler plot shows the generated idler (photons created, equal to the signal photons gained) and the idler that leaves the fiber. The difference is what the loss element must absorb.

Idler loss engineering. The idler loss can follow a spectral profile T(λi) between 0 and 1: flat, a band with loss inside it, or a lossless notch with loss outside it. Both continuous αi and the dB attenuation of each dump scale with T. Outside the lossless phase-matching band (|Δk| > 2Γ) a lossless fiber only oscillates, but a strongly damped idler still gives the signal the gain coefficient Γ²αi/((αi/2)² + Δk²). That wing gain is about 4Γ²L/αi, so it only matters next to a low peak gain. To judge the extension fairly, compare with a lossless fiber of equal peak gain, whose length is Lref = arccosh(√Gpeak)/Γ. With the default fiber this comparison gives: flat loss broadens the −3 dB band only once αi ≳ 3Γ (about 1.6× at 30 dB/m, but with 10 dB peak gain from 5 m). A lossless notch narrows the band when its edges fall inside the gain band, and changes nothing when all of the loss lies outside it. The reverse works: weak loss (about 3 dB/m) only at the centre of the lossless idler band lowers the peak and flattens the top. At equal peak gain that gives about 1.4× the bandwidth for roughly 4 dB less peak gain than the same fiber without loss (preset "Gain flattening"). Stronger centre loss opens a dip, which the readouts flag. With "Custom curve", control points (idler wavelength, fraction of peak loss) are joined by a monotone cubic that never overshoots 0–100 %. The points are fixed in absolute idler wavelength, so they stay put when λs or Δk change.

Optimiser. It minimises 10·[max(0, ln(target/R))]² + 0.05·[max(0, Gmin − G)]² − 0.01·G, where R is the continuous −3 dB width divided by the width of the phase-matched lossless reference and G is the peak gain in dB. Once the target ratio is met, it keeps raising the peak gain. With core radius and index step free, each evaluation rebuilds the fiber dispersion from LP01 solutions at Ω = 0, ±1 and ±2 THz (a quartic interpolant, within 10⁻⁴ rad/m of the exact model to ±4 THz). If mode sizes follow the geometry, Γ is rescaled by the LP01 overlap. A new fiber is only better if it is compared with the original one. So when the geometry or pump power is varied (or a fiber is picked on the map), the starting fiber is frozen as the reference baseline until you press "Use current fiber as reference". Fibers whose signal mode has V < 1.4 are penalised. In this silica step-index family the minimum GVM is about 5 ps/m (a ≈ 2.5–3 µm, Δn ≈ 0.04), compared with 15 ps/m for an SMF-like fiber, so geometry alone gives up to about 3× more bandwidth. The trade-off scan instead maximises R for each minimum peak gain (5–30 dB; below about 5 dB the −3 dB width is not meaningful), which maps the bandwidth that is achievable at each gain. Each evaluation solves the undepleted signal–idler pair on 301 wavelengths. The loss-curve points sit at fixed idler wavelengths spanning 2.5 × target × the reference width, and their heights are varied. The clearer benefit of a lossy idler is in the depleted regime and in tolerance to Δk: it suppresses back-conversion, so the pump keeps flowing into the signal (compare the photon-flux view with and without loss).

  • The coupling K uses n = 1.45 for all three waves. Across the spectrum, K scales as √(ωsωi) and the mode overlap Θ stays at its value at λs.
  • LP01 is the fundamental mode at all three wavelengths. At 532 nm the fiber is usually few-mode (V > 2.405), and other modes are ignored. Mode radii use Marcuse's Gaussian fit w/a = 0.65 + 1.619 V−3/2 + 2.879 V−6.
  • Brillouin and Raman scattering (switches; silica host in both modes). SBS uses the exact CW two-wave solution for pump and backward Stokes (Pp − PB constant, PB(L) = thermal seed h νp(kT/hνB)ΔνB, νB = 2nvA/λp, ΔνB = 20 MHz·(1550 nm/λp)²). Its pump loss gBPB(z)/Ap is applied in the coupled-wave run. SRS adds a forward Raman Stokes wave of the pump at the gain peak, seeded with hν·1 THz, and photon-conserving Raman terms dPk/dz = gR(Ω)PjPk/Ajk between pump, signal and idler, with Ajk = π(wj² + wk²)/2. In the spectral runs the pump loss into both Stokes waves is taken from the main run. The analytic curves and the optimiser leave scattering out.
  • Pump and signal are lossless. Without idler seed the gain does not depend on the input phases.
  • The sum Φp + Φs is conserved even with idler loss; its drift is reported as the numerical error.
  • Step size adapts to max(Γ√(1 + Φs/Φp), |Δk|, αi/2). The gain scan uses at most 20 000 steps per point.
