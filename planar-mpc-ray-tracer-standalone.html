Planar MPC Ray Tracer

Rays in planar (on-chip) multipass cells: smooth or faceted stadium, circle, segmented polygon and the integrated Herriott cell, two concave in-plane mirrors. Perturb any wall element, open the ports, and compare chaos, path statistics, re-entrance, mirror reflectance and fringe washout under angle dither.

Exit-angle error (left axis) and exit point from the window centre (right axis) of the centre ray. Grey: the ray does not come back through the window after N hits (it leaves earlier, later or past a mirror end).

Intensity and effective path

Centre beam. Intensity after j reflections (left axis, log) and the absorption-weighted path Leff(j) = Σ Iᵢ ℓᵢ accumulated so far (right axis). A weak absorber α reduces the integrated signal by α Leff.

Port statistics (Monte Carlo)

Rays start uniformly across the input port with angles uniform in θc ± θ₀ and stop at the output port (detected), the input port (lost), past an open wall or gap (leaked), or after the maximum number of reflections. Bars: power-weighted share of detected light per path bin, with R per reflection. Mean field: a fully chaotic cell, every chord the mean chord πA/P. Uses the port width and output position of the Ports panel (the window for the Herriott cell). Engine: planar_cell.trace_rays and evaluate.

Mirror reflectance over the cell's angles

Bars: share of the light's power arriving at the mirrors per 1° of |χ| (each hit weighted by the intensity it still carries), this cell. Grey outline: the same launch in the unperturbed cell. Line, right axis: mirror loss 1 − R(χ) of the DBR. Above the critical angle the first air gap reflects totally.

Every reflection uses the exact transfer-matrix R(χ) at the angle that ray meets. R per bounce is I_end^(1/n), the decay of the total power; ⟨R⟩ is weighted by the power arriving at each hit. Closed cell (ports ignored; rays that leave an open wall stop). Unperturbed cell: no random or single-element changes, no Herriott radius or spacing error. Mirror: bragg_grating.TrenchDBR, λ from the phase panel. Engine: cell_mirror on planar_cell.

Phase space at the mirrors

Each hit plotted as wall position s/P against sin χ. Regular orbits trace lines or points; chaotic ones fill areas. In the Herriott cell the two mirrors are the two halves of the s axis.

Twin-ray separation

The centre beam and a copy launched 1 nrad apart, ports closed. A straight rise on this log scale is exponential divergence (chaos, the smooth stadium); flat-faceted walls only grow linearly; a Herriott cell stays bounded (stable resonator). Dashed: Lyapunov fit per reflection.

Phase vs launch angle, and angle dither

Relative phase Δφp(θ) = k₀ n (Lp(θ) − Lp(θc))

Shaded band: the dither range ±A. Signed-log axis, so sub-wave slopes and mm-scale path jumps both show.

Visibility Vp against pass number

Dots: computed V. Dashed: linear-model prediction from the dither depth xp. Grey: share of dithered rays still on the centre ray's element path. Dotted: random-phase floor √π/(2√N).

Visibility against dither depth x = apA (every pass)

The curve is |J₀(x)| (sine) or |sinc(x)| (triangle). Amber: some rays switched element path. Hollow red: not resolved at the maximum sample count. Ringed: the pass shown.

Phase slope |ap| = k₀ n |dLp/dθ| against pass number

Horizontal lines: the slope at which the current amplitude A gives model V = 0.9, 0.5 and 0. Regular walls grow ∝ p, chaotic ones exponentially; a Herriott cell refocuses, so its slope oscillates without growing.

Vp = |time average of exp(iΔφp)| over one dither period: the share of an interference term between pass-p light and light that does not follow the dither that survives averaging. Ray picture, reflection phases ignored. Engine: ray_phase.dither_analysis on planar_cell; runs in a background worker.

Geometry, reflection law and analyses are a JavaScript port of misc-applets math-engines/engines/planar_cell (with ray_phase, cell_mirror and bragg_grating), checked against the Python engines' test vectors. Wall elements are flat facets or circular arcs; flat facets are extended 15 % past their corners; 2D ray optics with specular reflection.
