Er amplifier: heating along z

ΔT(z)

Two-level Er model with the overlaps of this cross-section (signal at 1532 nm, pump at the chosen wavelength), sputtered Al₂O₃:Er cross-sections (σa(1532) = 5.7×10⁻²¹ cm², τ = 7.5 ms, Cup = 4×10⁻¹⁸ cm³/s, 80 % radiative). Heat = absorbed − radiated: quantum defect, non-radiative decay, upconversion, quenched ions, and half of the background loss.

How it is computed
  • Cross-section heat flow: 2D finite volumes on a graded grid, substrate 500 µm thick with its bottom held at ambient, adiabatic sides at ±500 µm, nearly adiabatic top. The waveguide is long, so heat flows in the cross-section only (heat flow along z smooths features shorter than about the BOX thickness).
  • Mode: scalar finite-volume mode with every interface on a grid line; Γr = (nr/neff) ∫r|E|² / ∫|E|² and Δneff = ∫ n (dn/dT) ΔT |E|² / (neff ∫|E|²). High-contrast Si wires: TE and TM differ from the scalar result by up to ~10 %.
  • R′mode is the mode-weighted ΔT per W/m of heat; ΔT in K for 1 mW per mm of waveguide.
  • Heat: q′ = α eΔT/Ta P + β P²/Aeff + σ τ β P³ / (2hν Aeff²), Aeff the TPA area of the ridge, σ = 1.45×10⁻²¹ m² (Si). Use β = 0 for wide-gap materials (SiO₂, SiN, LN, AlGaAs with x ≥ 0.18).
  • Runaway: θ(ΔT) = ∫k/k₀ dT = R′ q′(ΔT); with k ∝ T−m this is exact when one material dominates. The threshold is the largest P for which a solution exists (for linear absorption alone, Pth = Ta / (e R′ α)).
  • Material data: the thermo_optic LUT (room temperature, 1550 nm). Thin-film conductivities and several dn/dT values are low-confidence; see math-engines/engines/thermo_optic/LUT.md.
